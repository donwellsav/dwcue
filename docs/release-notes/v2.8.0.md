# DonWells Cue 2.8.0

This release is about steady sound and picture during a show. Program now plays through one path you can meter and fade. Outputs recover by themselves. The clicks and gaps found in testing are gone. Video holds its frames and stays in step with the sound. A new Picture sync setting makes up for a video chain that delays the picture. Show files are stored exactly as in 2.7.

## Read this before updating

- **Shows move freely between 2.7 and 2.8.0.** Nothing about the show file has changed. Picture sync is saved on each machine, with its display and framing settings, not in the show.
- **Update a remote audio engine too.** Most of these fixes are in the engine, and the picture's sync uses a position only a 2.8.0 engine sends. The Pre-show check reports an app and engine of different versions.
- **The small picture of the output in the top bar is gone.** Copying the output for it once a second cost the audience dropped frames. The top bar still shows the output's status ("playing", the age of its last frame, held black). Clicking it still opens the Capture a picture panel.
- **MIDI controllers work again.** In 2.7.1 the app could not use MIDI controllers at all.

## Audio

- **Program has one path: Main, on the output you choose in Settings.** The Main meter and fader now show and control what the audience hears on that output. Before, they followed the computer's system default output even when another output was chosen, so on a computer like a Windows PC with Realtek chosen they stayed flat. The system default is no longer kept open when you have chosen another output. A cue set to its own output still plays there.
- **A chosen output stays put when the computer's default output changes.** For example, connecting an HDMI switcher or projector can make it the computer's default. Program keeps playing on the output chosen in Settings. "System Default" still follows the computer.
- **A lost output comes back by itself.** If an output drops out and returns, the app reopens it within about two seconds. Main and every cue on it play again without anyone choosing it again. Examples: a USB interface or HDMI device unplugged and replugged, an input switch, or the computer cutting the output off.
- **Stop All works even when no output is running,** for example while an output has dropped out. Before, it left cues fading forever.
- **No more clicks:**
  - at the loop point of a file that is not 48 kHz, such as a 44.1 kHz music bed;
  - when Stop All, or a GO with stop-all, lands on a cue that is already fading out;
  - when pausing and resuming. Both now ramp over 10 ms, and a paused cue still stops at the exact moment you pressed Pause;
  - on GO of a cue that is fading out;
  - when a playing QuickPlay is set to Restart and fired again;
  - when moving a fader, or switching mute or solo.
- **No dead air before the next cue.** When a cue played to the end of its file, the app waited its whole fade-out time over silence before Cue to Continue or a QuickPlay's Play Next started the next cue. The next cue now starts with the end of the file.
- **No hole in the music** when arming Cue to Continue on a playing loop, or trimming a playing cue's Out point.
- **Playback rides out a slow disk.** Each playing cue keeps about a quarter of a second decoded ahead, three times as much as before. Media on a USB drive or network share, or a busy computer, can pause the decoder that long without a gap. Video cues no longer read the picture data just to get their sound.
- **On a Mac, sound comes first.** The audio engine runs with the real-time priority macOS gives audio, and waveform analysis after opening a show runs in the background.

## Video

- **The picture holds its frames.** Video Output nudged the video's playback speed many times a second to keep it with the sound, and lost frames each time. It now corrects only real drift, at one steady speed. In testing, 1080p and HEVC video played with no dropped frames.
- **The picture starts in step with the sound and stays there.** It now follows the sound you hear, not the engine's position a little ahead of it. A start lands in step instead of catching up over the first seconds. Fades, pauses and resumes no longer make the picture jump, and jumps such as a loop land on time.
- **Cuts between video cues are immediate.** While a video cue plays, the next video cue loads and waits on its first frame, so the cut does not wait for the file to load. Every video start and seek also gets its first data sooner, which matters most on a USB drive or network share.
- **Stills cut without a flash of the standby picture,** and a cut from video to a still keeps the last video frame up until the still is ready.
- **The end of a video holds its last frame** instead of flashing the first frame. If playback fails during a cue, the picture is tried again instead of staying black for the rest of the cue.
- **On a Mac, a screen that drops out comes back live.** A switcher or extender re-syncing, or an input change, no longer closes the output and leaves seconds of black when the screen returns. The output is kept, playing in step, and shows again as soon as its screen is back. Meanwhile the operator sees "display missing" as before. On Windows the output still closes and reopens.
- **New: Picture sync.** In **Settings → Video Output → Picture sync**, set **Picture ahead of sound (ms)**, 0 to 500. The picture then runs that far ahead of the sound, making up for a switcher, scaler or projector that delays it while the sound goes straight to the desk. Measure the delay with the AV Sync test card; the operator's manual explains how.

## During a show

- **Edits during a show no longer interrupt playback.** Autosave runs shortly after any edit, a level nudge included. It used to rebuild the engine's whole show each time. During the rebuild a GO or an automatic follow could be refused as busy, and a playing cue could drop a moment of sound. It now just saves when nothing else has changed.
- **No hourly error.** The app checks for updates once, when it starts. Before, a built-in check an hour into a show threw an error.
- **A cue's first GO on its own output no longer waits behind the device list,** which the engine now refreshes every 5 seconds instead of every second.
- **The operator window uses less of the computer.** It no longer redraws the whole workspace 30 times a second.
- **Opening a large show for the first time is gentler.** Waveform requests go to the engine two at a time, so Stop All and saves are not held up behind them.

## What was checked, and what was not

- Every release build starts the packaged audio engine on Windows and macOS and requires it to answer as this version before an installer is published.
- On macOS, at 2.8.0:
  - the client checks and type checking, the 13 engine unit-test suites and the 104 engine network tests passed;
  - each fix above has a test that failed before the change;
  - a five-minute soak of the packaged app ran with no failures, no dropped frames and no audio underruns;
  - the packaged app played audio and video with every processor core busy and showed no effect;
  - before the work began, a four-hour soak of 2.7.1 set the baseline the changes were measured against.
- Not yet checked:
  - any of this on Windows;
  - a real projector, switcher or USB interface being unplugged and replugged;
  - media on a slow USB drive or network share;
  - a long soak of 2.8.0.

  Rehearse on the show machine with the show's own outputs and media before relying on it.
